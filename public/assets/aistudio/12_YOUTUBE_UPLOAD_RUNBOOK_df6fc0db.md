# YouTube Upload Runbook — Windows Desktop / YouTube Studio

**Purpose:** A practical manual fallback for uploading a finished zombie-story episode or Short from a Windows PC. The instructions reflect the official Help pages linked at the end and were checked on **29 October 2026**. YouTube may change its interface and eligibility rules; if a label differs, follow the current on-screen guidance and re-check the official links.

> **Important:** This runbook helps you complete the upload carefully; it cannot guarantee copyright clearance, policy approval, monetization, recommendations, views, or a flawless upload. Uploading a file and making it public are separate actions. For the first test, use **Private** or **Unlisted** and review it before any public release.

## 1. Prepare the finished files

For each long-form episode, have one final master video and its matching package ready. For each Short, prepare a separate, purpose-edited vertical/square video and its own title and metadata.

### Long-form episode package

- Final MP4 master, normally 16:9 (for example, 1920×1080), with working video/audio; H.264 video with AAC audio is a practical default used by the production profile. Confirm the file plays locally from start to finish.
- Final title (YouTube title limit: 100 characters).
- Description (limit: 5,000 characters) with an accurate spoiler-light summary, series/episode identifier, any required music/asset attributions, and real chapter timestamps only if verified against the final edit.
- Thumbnail file if eligible; use an original, accurate image and avoid graphic/misleading content.
- Captions/subtitles file (SRT/VTT) and the correct video language.
- Playlist choice, category, audience decision, and any age restriction.
- Rights/provenance notes for visuals, voice, music and sound effects.
- Decision on YouTube's altered/synthetic-content disclosure, based on the current policy and what viewers actually see.
- Intended visibility and, if scheduling, the confirmed date/time/timezone.

### Short package

- A separate, coherent vertical or square file, no longer than **3 minutes** under YouTube's current Shorts upload guidance.
- Separate Short title (100-character title limit), concise description, captions, thumbnail/frame choice if the account/UI supports it, audience and altered/synthetic disclosure decisions, rights records, and intended visibility.
- Check captions and text safe areas against the Shorts player interface. A Short should make sense on its own; don't upload a random crop of the long episode.

### Verify access before producing a large batch

- YouTube accounts are limited to uploads of up to 15 minutes by default; longer videos require account verification. Since an episode targeted at “about 15 minutes” can exceed the limit after the final edit, verify the channel/account beforehand or keep the final runtime within the displayed limit.
- Phone verification can unlock longer videos and custom thumbnails; other feature access and daily limits depend on the channel. Check **YouTube Studio → Settings → Channel → Feature eligibility** and the official feature-access page. Do not assume that ten uploads per day will be permitted.
- The YouTube Help upload guide says the upload window may save a video as private if it is closed before Visibility is selected. Keep the window open through the Visibility step and confirm the saved state afterward.

## 2. Start the upload in YouTube Studio

1. On the Windows PC, open <https://studio.youtube.com/> and sign in to the Google account that owns or manages the intended channel.
2. Check the channel name/avatar at the top-right. If you manage more than one channel, switch to the correct one before proceeding.
3. In the top-right, choose **CREATE → Upload videos**.
4. Select the finished MP4. For a multi-file upload, YouTube currently permits up to 15 videos at a time, but uploading one at a time is safer for a first release because each video needs its own review and settings.
5. Keep the browser/upload dialog open. Wait for upload progress and processing status; larger files and HD/4K processing can take time. Do not treat “upload complete” as “processing complete” or “published.”

Repeat these steps separately for the long episode and each Short. Do not put Shorts and long episodes under identical metadata by default.

## 3. Complete the Details page carefully

YouTube's upload flow may change, but the current Studio flow asks for video details before checks and visibility.

1. **Title:** paste the reviewed title; stay within the 100-character limit. Make it specific and honest, not an unsupported promise or bait-and-switch.
2. **Description:** paste the reviewed description. Verify all episode references, links, attributions, and chapter timestamps. Do not include a source or license claim unless verified.
3. **Thumbnail:** upload the custom thumbnail if the option is available. Keep it original, accurate, readable at small size, and within policy. For desktop, YouTube's current Help page lists a 50 MB limit and recommends 16:9 for videos and 9:16 for Shorts; re-check the page because dimensions and eligibility can change. Custom thumbnail access may require account verification. Shorts thumbnail options can differ from long-form and may be limited to the current Studio interface.
4. **Playlist:** choose the correct series/season playlist, or leave it blank until one exists.
5. **Audience (required):** answer whether the video is **made for kids** based on the intended audience and content. This is a compliance decision, not a general “safe/clean” label. Do not let the app or genre guess this for you.
6. Review **Age restriction** and apply it when appropriate. Do not try to use it as a substitute for following Community Guidelines.
7. Under **Show more**, review relevant fields:
   - **Altered or synthetic content:** decide whether the video contains realistic altered/synthetic content that requires disclosure under current YouTube policy. Stylized animation and realistic synthetic depictions are not automatically the same; assess the actual output and current guidance.
   - **Language and caption certification:** select the actual spoken language and any applicable caption details.
   - **Tags:** use only accurate, relevant terms. YouTube says tags generally play a minimal discovery role except for commonly misspelled terms; do not keyword-stuff or add unrelated tags.
   - **Category, comments/ratings, automatic chapters, embedding, notifications, and other advanced settings:** choose only what fits the channel. If chapters are added, confirm each timestamp matches the final rendered video.
   - **Paid promotion / branded content:** disclose when applicable.
8. Click **Next** to proceed.

## 4. Add captions and video elements

1. If the flow offers **Subtitles/Captions**, select the correct language and add/upload the matching SRT/VTT file or use the current Studio subtitle editor.
2. Check the opening, scene changes, speaker names, punctuation, timing, and ending. Captions generated by an automatic service can contain errors; don't assume they match the final audio without review.
3. Add an end screen or cards only if useful and supported by the account/video. YouTube Help currently says an end screen requires a video at least 25 seconds long.
4. For a Short, check that captions and important visuals stay readable in the vertical player; do not rely on a long-form end screen layout.
5. Click **Next**.

## 5. Review YouTube Checks

1. Wait for the **Checks** page to run available copyright checks. YouTube says checks can continue in the background, but for a cautious first release, wait for them to finish before selecting public visibility.
2. If a match or restriction appears, inspect it and review your asset/music rights. Replace/remove content or seek qualified advice as appropriate. Dispute only when you have a good-faith basis and supporting rights/evidence.
3. A clean result is **not a guarantee**: YouTube states copyright check results are not final, and claims or other issues may arise later. The check does not certify every policy requirement, license, or monetization outcome.
4. Use **Next** only after reviewing the result and deciding whether to proceed.

## 6. Choose Visibility — the critical human decision

The current Visibility screen may offer **Private**, **Unlisted**, **Public**, or **Schedule**. Read the displayed option and date/time before saving.

### Safest first test: Private

- Select **Private** to upload and inspect the actual YouTube version without making it public. A private video is not a public launch.
- Wait for processing; play the video in Studio and confirm picture, sound, captions, thumbnail, metadata, and intended audience/disclosure choices.
- If the version is wrong, correct the local master and re-upload or use Studio's available editor; do not assume a later edit repairs the original local package.

### Unlisted review

- Use **Unlisted** only if you intend link-based review and are comfortable with anyone holding the link being able to view it. Unlisted is not the same as private.
- Share the link only with intended reviewers. Get their feedback before making the video public.

### Publish now

- Choose **Public** only after the final human review: correct channel, correct master file, completed processing, title/description/thumbnail, captions, audience, AI disclosure, rights, checks, playlist, and release timing.
- Click **Publish/Save** only when you are prepared to make the video viewable under the displayed setting. Confirm the resulting status and URL in Studio.

### Schedule

- Select **Schedule**, then choose the intended date and time. Confirm the timezone shown by Studio and the selected channel; do not assume the production app's timezone or `Asia/Kolkata` is correct.
- A scheduled video normally remains private until the scheduled time. Check for channel restrictions/strikes that could affect the schedule, and verify the final scheduled status in Studio.
- Scheduling is still a public-release decision. Do not leave it to an unreviewed AI job.

Click **Save/Publish/Schedule** as appropriate, then return to Studio's Content page and verify that the video has the status you intended. If anything is ambiguous, leave it private and resolve it before proceeding.

## 7. Verify the result and record it

For each upload, record these details in the production project:

- Channel name and video title.
- Local master file path and checksum/version.
- YouTube video ID/URL, if assigned.
- Actual YouTube status: private, unlisted, public, or scheduled; and the displayed publish date/time/timezone if scheduled.
- Upload and processing state; date/time; checks status and any claims/restrictions.
- Captions, thumbnail, audience setting, synthetic-content disclosure, and playlist reviewed.
- Reviewer and approval record. Do not save OAuth tokens, passwords, or private recovery codes in the project.

If using an app's optional YouTube Data API instead of Studio, keep private-by-default and check the response **and** Studio afterward. Official YouTube pages currently conflict about whether unverified API projects are restricted to private viewing; do not rely on an assumed rule. The app must not report “public” unless the returned/observed status confirms it, and manual Studio upload is the fallback when API publishing is uncertain.

## 8. Troubleshooting checklist

- **Video is over 15 minutes:** verify the account/channel for longer uploads, then re-upload. Check feature eligibility and account status.
- **Custom thumbnail unavailable:** check phone verification/feature eligibility and current Studio instructions. Use a generated YouTube thumbnail only if appropriate.
- **Upload failed or is slow:** check file opens locally, available disk space, network stability, file format/size, browser version, and channel limits. Resume/retry only after checking whether the upload already created a video.
- **Processing takes time:** HD/4K versions may appear after SD; wait and inspect playback before release.
- **Copyright claim/restriction:** inspect the exact matched segment and your licenses. Replace or remove content where appropriate; don't dispute without evidence.
- **Unexpected private status:** check Studio's Content page, channel defaults, the saved Visibility choice, processing state, and any notice. API uploads must be checked against the actual response and Studio, not assumptions.
- **Daily upload limit:** limits apply across Studio, mobile, and API and vary by account/feature access. Wait or review current eligibility; do not repeatedly retry to evade a limit.
- **Upload dialog closed before visibility:** look in Studio → Content; the official guide says it may have saved as private. Verify rather than starting another upload blindly.
